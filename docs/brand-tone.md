# Brand Tone — Playful Learning Partners

## The rule of thumb
Write like a credentialed former educator talking to another education
professional — warm and direct, never cutesy. If a sentence would feel at
home on a preschool's Instagram caption, cut it. If it would feel at home in
a superintendent's inbox, keep it.

Playfulness in copy shows up as **confidence and specificity**, not
exclamation points, wordplay, or child-directed language. "Classroom-tested,
not YouTube-sourced" is the right register: it's a little pointed, a little
memorable, and entirely adult.

## Do
- Use concrete, specific claims: "CKLA-aligned supplements," "Title I
  funding-eligible engagements," "built by an active classroom teacher" —
  specificity reads as credible.
- Write in second person to the district ("your team," "your classrooms")
  and first person for Debra ("I've spent X years..."), not "we" language
  that implies a larger organization than exists.
- Keep sentences direct. One idea per sentence in headlines and CTAs.
- Let the pinwheel/color system carry visual playfulness so the copy doesn't
  have to try as hard — see brand-visual.md.

## Don't
- No exclamation points in headings or CTAs.
- No child-directed vocabulary ("kiddos," "fun-time," "adventure") in
  district-facing copy. ("Kiddos" is fine in Debra's own informal notes or
  internal docs — not on the public site.)
- No emoji anywhere on the production site.
- Don't oversell with superlatives ("the best," "revolutionary," "industry
  leading") — PLP's credibility argument is specificity and track record, not
  hype language, and hype invites the skepticism a new, unproven consultancy
  can't yet survive.
- Don't write copy that could be read as a claim about a specific district
  contract (see CLAUDE.md confidentiality section).

## Hard constraints (added 2026-07-12, confirmed by project owner)
These are non-negotiable and apply to ALL public site copy. When rewriting or
adding copy, check against this list before finalizing.

- **No em-dashes (—) or en-dashes (–) anywhere in site copy.** Restructure the
  sentence instead: use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses.
- **Never use these words:** game changer, unlock, leverage, dive into,
  unleash, revolutionary, maximize, treasure trove, delve, moreover, seamless,
  crucial, testament.
- **No "it's not just X, it's Y" construction** (or close variants like "not a
  break from learning, it is..."). State the positive claim directly.
- **No machine-like symmetrical lists.** Avoid rigid parallel triads where every
  item is the same length and shape. Vary sentence structure; let lists sound
  like a person wrote them, not a template.

## Example — same fact, two ways
- ❌ "Learning should be an adventure! Let's make school fun again!"
- ✅ "Play is one of the most reliable ways rigor actually sticks."

The second version is still distinctive and a little bold. It just isn't aimed
at a five-year-old, and it makes the claim directly instead of leaning on an
"it's not A, it's B" turn.
