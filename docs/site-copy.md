**What We Do**

**Built Around You**

### Grounded in the research

Our work is built on the Pedagogy of Play research from Harvard Graduate School of Education's Project Zero, a foundational framework in the field of playful learning.

That framework gives us four Playful Learning Indicators — Enjoyment, Curiosity, Empowerment, and Connection — that we use to plan lessons, structure classroom observations, and talk concretely with teachers and administrators about what's changing in a classroom.

The indicators are a shared language, not a rubric to impose. How they show up in a kindergarten studio is not how they show up in a fourth-grade writers theater, and we adjust accordingly.

## About — `/about`

### About Playful Learning Partners

Playful Learning Partners is an educational consulting practice built on a single conviction: that play belongs inside rigorous instruction, not alongside it.

We work with PreK–Grade 5 teams — teachers, paraprofessionals, coaches, and school leaders — to design systems that bring guided playful learning to life and keep it alive after the training ends. That work takes the form of professional learning, in-classroom coaching, classroom observation cycles, and consultation with administrators on how to sustain the practice.

Our focus is practical implementation. Storytelling and story acting. Community meetings. Centers and studios. Writers theater. Read aloud and text talk. Inclusive classroom structures that honor children's curiosity, creativity, and need to belong. These are the specific, teachable practices that turn a philosophy into something a teacher can do at 9:15 on a Tuesday.

We are especially committed to making playful learning accessible to every child and every educator — not only the schools with the budget for it. Whether we're facilitating a workshop, coaching a teacher in her own classroom, or sitting with a district leadership team, the goal doesn't change: help the adults build spaces where children can explore, connect, and thrive.

We welcome partnerships with schools, districts, nonprofits, and community organizations willing to reconsider what learning can look like when we trust the power of play.

### The research behind the work

Playful Learning Partners' approach draws on the Pedagogy of Play research developed by Harvard Graduate School of Education's Project Zero.

Project Zero's Playful Learning Indicators — Enjoyment, Curiosity, Empowerment, and Connection — give us a way to describe what playful learning actually looks like when it's working, and to notice when it isn't. We use them to guide implementation and to give teachers and administrators something concrete to point at.

We also treat them as flexible. Each school's culture is different, and the indicators bend to fit the building.

## Services — `/services`

Page intro: Services are selected à la carte. Professional development and in-classroom coaching are built to work together, but there's no required package — we build around your goals, your staffing, and your funding.

### Workshops

Interactive, hands-on sessions built for the teams who'll be doing the work together. Schools and districts select from our workshop topics based on their goals, staffing, and implementation priorities.

Every workshop includes:

* Interactive activities and collaborative planning time
* Case studies and classroom scenarios
* Implementation tools and templates
* Discussion protocols and reflection activities
* Practical, immediate classroom application
* Participant resources to support the work between sessions

All participants leave with planning tools, templates, and materials aligned to the focus areas — designed to support ongoing implementation and collaboration across co-teaching teams and inclusive classrooms.

Workshop topics: Pedagogy of Play · Community Meeting · Storytelling & Story Acting · Writers Workshop · Read Aloud / Text Talk · Centers & Studios · Paraprofessional Professional Development

### Classroom Coaching

Workshops start something. Coaching is what makes it stick.

We recommend ongoing classroom observation and feedback cycles across the school year, intentionally spaced so teams have real time to try a strategy, adjust it, and reflect before the next cycle.

Each cycle includes:

* Classroom observation
* Co-teaching and modeling
* Pre-planning conversations and structured reflection
* Actionable recommendations
* Debrief conversations
* Support with the materials each practice requires

### Administrator Collaboration & Sustainability

Regular working sessions with school and district leaders on what's changing in classrooms, what teachers and students need next, and how to build a model that carries the work forward without us.

### Monthly PLC Meetings

Standing sessions for all teachers involved in the work — what's working, where they want help, and dedicated time to brainstorm and collaborate vertically across grade levels.

### Family Engagement Nights

An evening where students and teachers show families what playful learning looks like from the inside. PLP helps coordinate the event and prepares teachers and students to engage families in a way that's genuinely playful — not a presentation about play, an experience of it.

### Funding & Grant Support

Budget shouldn't be the reason this doesn't happen.

We help schools and districts identify funding sources, develop grant applications, and communicate with granting organizations to support playful learning work.

## FAQ — `/faq`

**Will this work with the curriculum we already have?**
Yes — that's the design. We don't ask schools to replace what they're teaching. Playful learning practices are built to sit inside your existing curriculum, using the structures and standards you already work from. Our lessons are developed with the Massachusetts state frameworks in view, and we return to grade-level standards as we build.

**Some of our teachers already feel like they're doing playful learning. What would they get out of this?**
Often quite a lot, and usually not what they expect. Teachers who already value play tend to be doing it well in moments and inconsistently across the week. The work here is structural — how a community meeting is run so every child talks, how a studio is set up so the materials do some of the teaching, how story acting connects to what you're assessing on Friday.

**What is playful learning, and how is it different from just play?**
Playful learning is guided. There's an instructional purpose behind the setup, the materials, and the teacher's role in it. Children experience choice, wonder, and joy; the teacher is working toward specific understanding. The difference isn't in how it looks to a child — it's in what the adult is doing while it happens.

**How heavy is the lift for teachers?**
Front-loaded. The practices are designed to work inside the schedule teachers already have rather than adding to it. The initial adjustment is real — new structures always are — which is why we recommend pairing workshops with in-classroom coaching instead of leaving teachers to figure it out alone.

**What grade levels do you work with?**
PreK through Grade 5.

**Do we need to buy expensive new materials?**
Most practices require little or nothing new. Centers and studios is the one that asks for more, and even there we start by finding what's already in your building. Reusing and repurposing existing materials is part of how we work, not a compromise.

**How do you align play with academic standards?**
Massachusetts state frameworks are the reference point. As we build lessons and practices with your teams, we work back to grade-level standards — so what a teacher does during a studio block connects to what they're accountable for.

**Does this work in large classes?**
Yes. The practices scale with class size; what changes is how you structure grouping and rotation, which is part of what coaching addresses.

**How will we know it's working?**
We use Project Zero's Playful Learning Indicators — Enjoyment, Curiosity, Empowerment, and Connection — as the shared framework for classroom observation and debrief conversations. That gives teachers and administrators specific, observable things to look for and talk about, rather than a vague sense that the room feels different. Administrator sessions are where we review what's changing and what needs to change next.

**Can we customize which services we receive?**
Yes. Services are selected à la carte, and the Playful Learning Indicators we emphasize can be weighted toward your school's priorities. A building focused on belonging and behavior will emphasize different indicators than one focused on early literacy engagement.

**We're not sure we can fund this. What are our options?**
Talk to us anyway. We help schools identify funding sources and support grant applications, and we are willing to work with schools and districts at any level of need.
