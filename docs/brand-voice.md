# Brand Voice — Playful Learning Partners

## Core thesis
"Bringing play into practice." PLP's differentiator from free content
(YouTube, Pinterest, generic teacher blogs) is that every strategy and
material is classroom-tested: refined through Debra's hands-on coaching work
inside real classrooms alongside teachers, not assembled the night before a
training. This claim should be present, directly or indirectly, on every
page — it is the whole argument for paying for this instead of Googling it.

**FACTUAL CORRECTION (2026-07-12, from Matt): Debra is NOT a classroom
teacher. Never describe her as "an active classroom teacher," "an active
teacher," or similar. She is a playful learning coach/consultant who works
directly in classrooms with teachers and students. "Classroom-tested" is
fine; "by a classroom teacher" is false.**

## Positioning statement
"A boutique provider of guided playful learning pedagogy."

Use this framing (or close variants) deliberately. It does three things at once:
- **Boutique** implies agility and a custom menu of services per district —
  the opposite of a large, federally-funded, rigid-cohort program.
- **Guided** signals expertise and structure, not unstructured free play.
- **Pedagogy** signals curriculum literacy to an education-sector buyer, not
  a consumer-facing "activities" brand.

Avoid "industry leader" or similar superlative claims — they invite scrutiny
PLP can't yet back up (no long client roster, business is new), and they read
as generic marketing copy rather than substantiated positioning.

## Competitive differentiation (internal reference — do not name competitors
## on the public site)
- **Playworks** — recess/organized-play focused. Some schools may confuse PLP
  with this; the site should make clear PLP is about instructional strategy
  and curriculum integration, not recess programming, without ever naming
  Playworks directly.
- **Playmaker University** — social-emotional/trauma-informed, grant-funded,
  cohort-based. PLP is not grant-application-gated and is not trauma-focused;
  the "boutique," pay-for-service framing is the differentiator here.
- Do not build comparison tables or "unlike X, we..." copy referencing named
  competitors. Differentiation should be implicit in how PLP describes its
  own model (boutique, customized, classroom-tested), not explicit contrast.

## The three pillars — how to talk about each right now
1. **District consulting** — the only live, sellable service. This gets the
   real estate: homepage, the "For Districts" page, testimonials, CTA.
2. **Teacher membership repository** — not operational. If mentioned at all
   pre-launch, one sentence, framed as "in development," no pricing, no
   sign-up mechanism implied.
3. **Keepers of Play** (parents/homeschoolers) — same as above: mention only
   as forthcoming, never build out functionality for it in this phase.

Do not give pillars 2 and 3 visual or copy weight proportional to pillar 1.
A curriculum director should read this site and immediately understand what
Debra does *today*, not get an equally-weighted tour of three future products.

## Audience
Primary: district administrators, curriculum directors, Title I coordinators.
These are the people this whole site is written for. Copy should assume a
professional reader making a procurement-adjacent decision, not a parent
browsing for activity ideas.

## What PLP is not
- Not a daycare or early-childhood-activities brand
- Not a grant-application-only program
- Not a recess/organized-play vendor
- Not free, crowdsourced, or unvetted content
